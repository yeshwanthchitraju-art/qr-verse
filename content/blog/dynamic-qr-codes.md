If you've ever printed a QR code on a menu, a flyer, or a storefront window, you already know the problem: once it's printed, you can't change where it goes.

That's the core limitation of a **static QR code**. The destination URL is baked into the code itself. If your menu changes, your promotion ends, or your website moves, you have to reprint everything.

## What is a dynamic QR code?

A dynamic QR code doesn't encode your final destination. Instead, it encodes a short redirect link — something like `qrverse.app/r/abc123`. When someone scans it, the server looks up where that short link should go and redirects them instantly.

Because the mapping lives on the server, you can change the destination at any time. The printed QR never changes.

## Why this matters

Here's what dynamic QR codes unlock:

- **Update your destination anytime.** Change the URL behind your QR without reprinting a single sticker.
- **Track every scan.** Because the redirect goes through your server, you can count scans, record devices, and see where your audience is.
- **Fix mistakes.** Misspelled the URL when you generated the code? No problem — just update the destination. No reprint.
- **Schedule changes.** Run a weekend promotion? Point the QR at a special page on Friday and switch it back Monday.

## The tradeoff

Dynamic QR codes require an internet connection to redirect (the server has to be reached). Static QR codes work offline because the destination is encoded directly. For 99% of marketing use cases, your customer's phone is online anyway — so this is rarely a real limitation.

## When to use static vs dynamic

Use **static** QR codes for permanent, unchanging destinations where you'll never need analytics — like a Wi-Fi password card or a fixed product manual link.

Use **dynamic** QR codes for everything else: menus, promotions, business profiles, contact pages, event details. If there's any chance the destination might change, go dynamic.
