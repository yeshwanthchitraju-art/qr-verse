Most QR code generators show you a single number: total scans. That's better than nothing, but it barely scratches the surface. If you want to understand what's actually happening when people scan your QR codes, you need to go deeper.

## The metrics that matter

### Total scans and unique visitors

Total scans tells you how many times your QR was scanned. Unique visitors (estimated by hashed IP) tells you how many distinct people scanned. The gap between the two tells you whether the same people are scanning repeatedly.

### Scans over time

A single number hides the story. Scans plotted over time shows you when interest spikes — and whether your campaign is building momentum or fading.

### Device and platform breakdown

Knowing that 80% of your scans come from iPhones tells you your audience is mobile-first (as expected). But the split between iOS and Android, and the browser they use, can reveal whether your landing page is optimized for the right devices.

### Location

Where are your scans coming from? Country and city data helps you understand your reach — and whether your local marketing is actually reaching locals.

### Referrers

If someone scans your QR from a shared link (not the printed code), the referrer tells you where the traffic came from. This is rare for QR codes but valuable when it happens.

## What to do with the data

Analytics without action is just a dashboard. Here's how to turn metrics into decisions:

- **If scans are low**, your QR might be placed poorly. Try a more visible location, a larger size, or a call-to-action label like "Scan for menu."
- **If scans are high but landing page views are low**, your redirect might be slow or broken. Test it yourself.
- **If scans are high but button clicks are low**, your landing page isn't converting. Simplify the page, make buttons more prominent, and cut anything that isn't essential.
- **If one location dominates**, double down on that market. Run local promotions and place more QR codes there.
